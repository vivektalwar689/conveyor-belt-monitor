# Design document: monitoring and failure prediction for a multi-kilometre conveyor

This is the long-form design. The [README](../README.md) is the summary. Numbers quoted here come from
`ml/artifacts/metrics.json`, `vision/artifacts/*.json` and the tests, all regenerated by the commands in the
README.

---

## 1. Context and assumptions

The brief gives the environment (steep inclines, sharp turns, heat, dust, vibration, abrasive load, 18 h/day duty)
but not the machine. Where it was silent I assumed something plausible and wrote it down:

| Item | Assumption | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Conveyor | Single-flight overland conveyor **CV-01**, 6.0 km centre-to-centre, steel-cord belt 1,600 mm wide, **5 m/s**, 6,000 t/h design | Sets loop length (12,050 m), loop period (~40 min) and how fast material moves past sensors |
| Route | 8 % incline (km 1.2–2.4), 5 % incline, 6 % decline, two horizontal curves (R 1,500 m and R 1,200 m) | Stress differs along the route, so failure rates and sensor density do too |
| Drives | Head drive, 3 × 1 MW, gravity take-up near the head, loading point at the tail | One drive means one gearbox, which means very few gearbox failures to learn from |
| Duty | Runs 06:00–24:00 site time (IST), **planned stop 00:00–06:00** daily, plus random trips | The planned stop is when maintenance happens, so "predict early enough to use the next stop" is the goal |
| Climate | Hot and dusty: ambient 20–40 °C daily cycle plus weather drift; dust storms degrade radio links | Temperature must be judged relative to ambient; dust is the main data-loss driver |
| Protection | Rip detectors, pull-wires, sway and slip switches already exist and trip the belt in seconds | We are the **predictive** layer (hours–weeks). We do not replace hard-wired protection, and nothing here sits in a safety loop |
| Time scales | Degradation is **compressed** in the simulator (bearing P-F interval median ~6 days running, plus 20 % "fast" failures of ~1 day) | Keeps simulations tractable. Real P-F intervals for idler bearings are often weeks to months |

## 2. What can fail, and what we monitor

The prioritisation is frequency × consequence × detectability:

| Component | Failure mode | Consequence | Measurable precursor (earliest first) | Sensor in this design | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idler / pulley bearings (~14,000 rollers on 6 km) | Wear → seizure | A seized roller is cut by the belt and can rip it. Friction heat is a fire risk. Unplanned stop. **The most common conveyor failure.** | HF acceleration envelope → vibration RMS → temperature → noise/smoke (the P-F curve) | Accelerometer (edge computes RMS + envelope), bearing housing temperature | ML on the station population |
| Belt tracking | Mistracking | Edge damage, spillage, belt rubbing the structure, sway-switch trip | Lateral offset growing over days (often worse on an empty belt and on curves) | Belt-edge (ultrasonic) position sensor | ML + offset limits |
| Drive gearbox | Gear/bearing wear | Long outage (swap a spare gearbox) | Housing vibration, oil temperature **in excess of what load and ambient explain**, motor current | Accelerometer, oil RTD, motor current | Trend-to-threshold |
| Drive pulley lagging | Lagging wear → slip | Belt glazing, fire risk, trips at high load | Slip % between driven and non-driven pulley, **only visible under load** | Two speed encoders | Trend-to-threshold |
| Belt surface | Longitudinal rip, tear, hole, puncture, impact damage, cover wear | A rip can destroy kilometres of belt in minutes. Wear sets belt life | Visible damage | Camera on the return strand + RFID tags in the splices | Detector + belt-coordinate tracking |
| Motor | Winding over-temperature | Trip | Winding temperature | RTD | Absolute limits |
| Take-up | Belt stretch until take-up travel runs out | Slip and the need to re-splice | Carriage position trend | Position sensor | Displayed only (forecast not built) |

**Deliberately not modelled:** fire (CO / linear heat detection), splice integrity (magnetic steel-cord
scanners), cover thickness (ultrasonic), chute blockage and spillage, acoustic idler monitoring, drones and
inspection robots. Most of these are valuable, but they are either separate products or would multiply the
simulator without changing the architecture.

## 3. Sensor suite and placement

Instrumenting all ~14,000 rollers individually is possible (wireless smart idlers, fibre-optic distributed
temperature/acoustic sensing) but expensive. The brief talks about "sensor points", so the design uses **24
sensor stations**. Each station is an edge node with a suite of sensors monitoring the idler sets or pulleys
around it, placed where stress or consequence is highest:

| Zone | Stations | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Tail + loading zone | S01–S02 | Impact idlers take the falling ore. Off-centre loading starts mistracking. A belt scale gives throughput |
| Flat carry | S03, S04, S10, S15, S20, S21 | Baseline coverage roughly every 300–600 m |
| 8 % / 5 % inclines, decline | S05–S09, S14, S16 | High belt tension and idler load |
| Horizontal curves | S11–S13, S17–S19 | The belt wants to run to the inside or outside, so mistracking risk is highest here |
| Head | S22–S24 | Transition idlers, take-up and bend pulleys, drive (motor, gearbox, pulley speed) |

Channels per station type (130 channels in total):

* every station: `vib_rms` (mm/s), `vib_env` (g), `bearing_temp`, `ambient_temp` (°C), `belt_offset` (mm);
* tail: `belt_speed` (non-driven pulley encoder); loading: `load_tph` (belt scale);
* take-up: `belt_tension`, `takeup_pos`;
* drive: `motor_current`, `motor_temp`, `gearbox_temp`, `gearbox_vib`, `drive_speed`, `belt_speed`.

**Edge computes features, not waveforms.** An accelerometer samples at kHz. The edge node reduces each
5-minute window to RMS and envelope values and sends those (37,440 readings/day, ~3 MB/day of JSON). A mine
radio network can carry that. Raw waveforms stay on the edge for on-demand diagnosis. The simulator therefore
emits 5-minute aggregates and does not simulate raw vibration.

## 4. Data path

```
sensors ──► edge node (per station) ──► radio gateway GW-1/2/3 ──HTTP JSON──► FastAPI /api/ingest
            5-min aggregates            store-and-forward buffer             │
                                        at-least-once delivery               ▼
camera ───► edge node (YOLO, RFID) ────────────────────────────────► /api/vision/pass
CMMS ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► /api/maintenance
                                                                         │
                                         SQLite (WAL): readings, dead_letter, anomalies,
                                         predictions, station_status, vision_* ◄── analysis worker
                                                                                   (separate process)
                                                                         │
                                                         dashboard (GET /api/*, auto-refresh)
```

The API server and the analysis worker are separate processes. In one process, ingestion requests and the
analysis cycle competed for Python's GIL and intake slowed to a quarter of the target rate. They share only the
SQLite database (WAL mode, short write transactions).

Message format (per gateway batch):

```json
{"gateway_id": "GW-2", "sent_at": "2026-11-14T10:05:00+00:00",
 "readings": [{"station_id": "S12", "sensor": "belt_offset", "ts": 1794650400000, "value": 41.7, "unit": "mm", "seq": 81233}]}
```

`readings` is keyed by `(station_id, channel, ts)`, so ingestion is **idempotent**. A retried batch changes
nothing. `seq` is a per-station counter, which makes gaps from packet loss observable.

## 5. Messy data: what goes wrong and what the system does

The simulator injects every problem below at a known rate and records ground truth for it. The backend handles
it with one rule: **never silently guess**. Each reading is either rejected with a reason, stored with a "bad"
flag (kept for audit, excluded from analytics), or stored clean, possibly with an informational flag.

| Problem (where it comes from) | Example in the simulator | Handling | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heterogeneous timestamps | GW-1 ISO `Z`, GW-2 epoch-ms, GW-3 ISO `+05:30` | Parse all three into UTC epoch. **Naive timestamps are rejected**, because local vs UTC is a 5.5 h guess. Future (> 10 min) and stale (> 30 d) timestamps are rejected | `ingest.py` |
| Non-SI units | °F at S07, in/s at S15, K at S24 | Convert to canonical units with a flag. Incompatible units (e.g. "mm" for a temperature) are rejected | `ingest.py` |
| Legacy PLC tag names | `GBX_OIL_T`, `MTR_CURRENT_A` at the drive | Alias map, flagged | `ingest.py` |
| Error codes | -999, 9999, 6553.5 (0xFFFF/10), `"ERR"`, `null` | Value set to NULL with a flag | `ingest.py` |
| Physically impossible values | Bearing at 400 °C | Kept and flagged out-of-range, never used | `ingest.py` |
| Duplicates (at-least-once delivery) | 2 % of batches resent | Dropped in-batch and by the primary key | `ingest.py`, schema |
| Packet loss, station and **gateway** outages, dust storms | 8 stations dark when a gateway fails | Station shown as **"No data"**, never "Normal". Features record completeness, and confidence drops when it is low | `anomaly.py`, `features.py` |
| Late, out-of-order data (store-and-forward) | Hours of data arriving at once | Accepted and flagged late. Each analysis cycle **recomputes** from the last 8 days, so late data is simply included next time | `analysis.py` |
| EMI spikes | ×3–8 isolated outliers | Flagged if far from **both** neighbours. A start/stop step is not a spike, because the next sample agrees with it | `quality.py` |
| Stuck sensors | Value frozen for hours | ≥ 1 h of identical values on a channel that always carries noise. Speed, current and load are exactly 0 when stopped, so they are only judged while running | `quality.py` |
| Calibration drift | Temperature reading creeps up 1.5–4 °C/day | A temperature rise with **no** vibration change is labelled *suspected sensor drift* (a data-quality issue), not a bearing fault | `anomaly.py` |
| Missing values in features | Gaps | Gradient boosting handles NaN natively. **No forward-fill or interpolation**: forward-filling a failing bearing's temperature hides the trend | `features.py` |

## 6. Anomaly detection

Signals are normalised twice before any threshold is applied (`backend/features.py`):

1. **Peer-relative.** At every 5-minute step a station is compared with the median of all stations: log ratio
   for vibration, difference for temperature rise over ambient. Weather, dust, load surges and start-ups hit all
   stations at once and cancel out.
2. **Frozen self-baseline.** That peer-relative signal is compared with the station's own baseline, built
   sequentially from *accepted* hours only. An hour joins the baseline 24 h later, and only if it was not
   anomalous at the time. While a station degrades its baseline therefore freezes at the last healthy level.
   *(The first version used a rolling 6-day median. On long degradations the fault leaked into its own baseline
   and the z-score collapsed right before failure. The fix was found by plotting a failure end to end; see
   `docs/images/example_bearing.png`.)* If a station has looked anomalous for 7 days the baseline re-learns, which
   covers a component swap nobody logged.

The result is a robust z-score that means "unusual compared with itself and with the others right now". It is
comparable across stations, so one rule and one model work everywhere. Rules on top (`backend/anomaly.py`):

| Type | Opens when | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Bearing HF envelope | z ≥ 4 | low/medium/high at z 4/7/12 |
| Bearing vibration | z ≥ 4 or RMS ≥ 4.5 mm/s (ISO 10816 zone C) | high in zone D (≥ 7.1) |
| Bearing temperature | (z ≥ 4 or ≥ 75 °C) **and** vibration corroborates | high at ≥ 85 °C or ≥ 35 °C over ambient |
| Suspected sensor drift | temperature z ≥ 5 with **no** vibration change | low (data quality) |
| Belt mistracking | ‖offset‖ ≥ 40 mm | low/medium/high/critical at 40/60/80/95 mm (sway switch at 110) |
| Gearbox, slip, motor | absolute limits (4.5/7.1 mm/s, 85/95 °C, 1/2 % slip, 130/150 °C) | medium/high |
| Station offline, sensor stuck, noisy | data-quality conditions | low/medium |

Lifecycle: an anomaly **opens** when its condition holds in 3 of the last 4 samples. It **stays open** while a
lower "hold" level is met (z ≥ 3 vs. 4 to open) and **clears** after 12 consecutive normal samples (1 h). That is
hysteresis in both level and time. Equipment anomalies are frozen while the belt is stopped: a bad bearing does
not "recover" overnight.

## 7. Prediction: what, where, when

**Framing.** For each (station, failure mode) at every running hour: P(failure within **72 h**) plus a time to
failure. 72 h means "can it wait for one of the next three planned stops?"

**Two methods, chosen by data availability:**

* **ML for bearings and mistracking.** About 24 stations share the same physics, so a failure anywhere is a
  training example for every station. Model: `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` on ~24 features (peer/self
  deviations over 1/6/24 h, z-scores, 24 h and 72 h slopes, absolute levels, load, run fraction, data
  completeness, station stress). Time to failure: three quantile regressors (P10/P50/P90) on log-hours.
* **Trend-to-threshold for the drive.** There is one gearbox and one drive pulley, so years of operation would
  yield a handful of failures: not a training set. The health indicator is load-normalised: gearbox vibration and
  oil temperature *in excess* of a regression on load fitted over a known-good period, and slip normalised to
  full load. An exponential trend is fitted to that excess. The trend must be statistically significant (slope
  t-stat ≥ 3) and persistent, and is extrapolated to an alarm limit with bootstrap uncertainty. No labels are
  needed. Degradation only advances while the belt runs, so trends are fitted in running hours and converted to
  calendar time with the duty schedule.

**Training data and splits.** Five independent simulated belt-years of 120 days, 24 stations each: train on
seeds 11 and 12, choose thresholds and calibration on seed 13, test on seed 21 (normal) and seed 22 (**harsh**:
~5× packet loss, outages, stuck/drifting sensors, spikes, error codes). The split is **by run, never by row**.
Neighbouring hours of one degradation are near-duplicates, the same leakage problem as the vision dataset.
Features are produced by the *production* ingest → clean → feature code (no train/serve skew).

**Threshold and persistence.** The threshold is chosen on the validation run as the setting that catches most
failures ≥ 24 h ahead while keeping false alarms ≤ 4 per 1,000 station-days (about one false alarm every 10 days
on this belt). Alarm fatigue is the main way predictive maintenance systems die. An alert needs **two consecutive
hourly decisions** above the threshold. The live system applies exactly the rule the evaluation measured: an
early version flipped a healthy station to "predicted failure" on a single 5-minute reading that crossed the
(deliberately low) threshold.

**Calibrated ETA bands.** The raw P10–P90 bands from the quantile regressors were **overconfident**: on alerted
test rows they covered only ~53 % of actual failure times instead of 80 %. Consecutive hours of one degradation
are near-duplicates, so the trees fit trajectories too tightly. I fixed this with **conformalized quantile
regression** on the validation belt-year: widen the band in log-hours by the 80th percentile of the validation
miss. Test coverage is now 82 % for bearings (92–95 % for mistracking). The coverage guarantee is approximate,
because validation rows are correlated within a degradation.

**Evaluation is event-level.** Row AUC says little about a maintenance alarm. The evaluation replays hourly
decisions over the held-out belt-years and asks, per failure, whether it was predicted, how far ahead, and
whether early enough to fix in a **planned** stop. Per alert episode, it asks whether the component really
failed within 10 days. Results are in the [README](../README.md#results).

## 8. Vision: belt wear and tear on a continuous loop

### 8.1 Dataset and detector
See the README for source and rationale. The audit (`vision/audit.py`) checks labels and finds exact duplicates
(MD5), flipped/rotated near-duplicates (dihedral-invariant perceptual hash) and same-scene frames (CNN embedding
cosine ≥ 0.97, threshold chosen by looking at pairs in each similarity band). It merges these into 138 scene
groups and re-splits 70/15/15 by group, with multi-label stratification so every class appears in every split.
The detector is YOLO11n (COCO-pretrained, 512 px, CPU-trained), chosen because the edge must keep up with
~5 frames/s.

### 8.2 Edge/backend split
The **edge node** (`vision/edge_sim.py`) runs the detector next to the camera and sends one compact report per
revolution: odometer readings of RFID tag reads, the odometer ranges it actually imaged, and detections with
160 px JPEG crops. Images never cross the radio link. The **backend** (`backend/vision_service.py`) does
localisation and multi-pass association, which need history and are cheap.

### 8.3 Consequences of the loop, and handling

| # | Consequence of a continuous loop | Handling in this system | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The same defect passes the camera every ~40 min: per-frame alerting gives ~27 duplicate alerts per defect per day | Detections associate to persistent **defect tracks**; one defect is one alert | Built, tested |
| 2 | "Where" is a place *on the belt*, not on the structure | Positions are kept in **belt coordinates** (metres after a splice) | Built |
| 3 | Dead reckoning from belt speed drifts: a 0.4 % encoder error is ~48 m per revolution and unbounded over time | **RFID tag per splice**: every read re-zeroes position, and tag-to-tag distance re-scales the odometer per segment (also absorbs belt stretch, slip, missed reads) | Built, measured |
| 4 | Coordinates wrap: 12,049 m and 1 m are 2 m apart | Circular distance and circular mean throughout | Built, tested |
| 5 | One look is not proof: carry-back, water and dust look like damage once | **Confirm** after sightings on ≥ 2 revolutions at the same belt position; unconfirmed tracks missed twice are dropped | Built, measured |
| 6 | "Not seen" only counts if that stretch was imaged (fouled lens, skipped frames, stops) | Misses counted only inside the pass's **coverage**. A confirmed defect imaged 3× without detection is marked *repaired*, not deleted | Built, tested |
| 7 | Repeated sightings form a time series | Per-sighting size, growth rate (mm/day), severity escalation for growing or long tears | Built (see limitation below) |
| 8 | The per-frame false-positive rate is multiplied by ~12,000 frames per revolution | Measured, and the operating point chosen with that multiplication in mind (§8.4) | Analysed |
| 9 | When the belt stops, a fitter needs to know where on the 6 km structure to walk | "If stopped now": belt coordinate under the camera → strand + chainage of each defect | Built |
| 10 | The loop gives ~12 km of fresh belt imagery every 40 min | Should be used for hard negatives and self-supervision (a defect persists, dirt does not) | Not built: next step |
| 11 | Re-splicing or replacing a segment renumbers the belt | Belt register update and track migration | Not built |
| 12 | Carry side vs. pulley side, belt turnover, motion blur at 5 m/s, lighting | Camera on the return strand after the cleaners (empty, cleaner belt), simulated blur and light; turnover not handled | Partly |

### 8.4 Evaluation
Method (`vision/loop_eval.py`):
* **Setup.** The simulated edge node runs for 30 revolutions over a register of 25 splices (segment lengths vary
  ±10 %) on a belt 0.15 % longer than its record.
* **Errors injected.** The odometer has +0.4 % bias with a random walk. 5 % of RFID reads are missed. 15 % of
  passes have a 0.8–2.5 km imaging gap.
* **Defects.** 11 true defects, one appearing at revolution 8 and one repaired at revolution 14. Each true defect
  is always the same held-out test image, re-rendered each revolution with different light, motion blur, noise
  and lateral wander.
* **Distractors.** Each revolution adds 40 healthy frames (6 defect-free test images plus crops of test images away
  from labelled boxes) and 4 frames with synthetic carry-back.
* **Scoring.** Ground truth for each defect is every labelled box in its image, mapped to the belt the same way as
  detections.

Results (baseline detector, confidence floor 0.25):

| | RFID tags | Dead reckoning per pass | No fiducials |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median / p95 localisation error | 0.02 m / 0.08 m | 46 m / 72 m | 1.1 km / 2.3 km (2.4 km at the last pass) |
| Defects confirmed | 11/11 | 1/11 | 0/11 |
| Confirmed tracks in total | 31 | 100 | 80 |

Dead reckoning does not just mislocate defects. Because the error changes between passes, detections of one
defect scatter, while unrelated detections coincide; both failure modes produce confident, wrong tracks.

Tracker parameters: gates ±2.5 m along the belt and ±0.30 m across; confirm at 2 sightings on different passes; drop
a tentative track after 2 imaged misses; mark a confirmed track repaired after 3 imaged misses; ignore detections
below a 0.35 confidence floor (deployed detector). Lowering the gate is
possible because tag-based localisation is good to ~0.1 m; the gate is wider than that because box centres
jitter.

Operating point (baseline detector). Replaying the same reports with a higher per-detection confidence floor
trades recall for precision at the track level: 11/11 defects with 16 phantom tracks at 0.25, 9/11 with 8 at 0.35,
8/11 with 4 at 0.45, 7/11 with 1 at 0.55. The per-frame false-positive rate on healthy belt (45 % at 0.25, 7.5 % at 0.55, 1.9 % at
0.65) multiplied by ~12,050 frames per revolution is the binding constraint for a real deployment (§12). The fix is
data, not tracking logic: site-specific healthy footage as hard negatives, which the loop provides continuously.

**Hard-negative experiment.** The dataset has 39 defect-free images, so the detector had barely seen healthy
belt. I mined 1,199 defect-free crops from *training* images (regions with no labelled box, 4 % margin) and
fine-tuned the baseline for 8 epochs at a low learning rate. Box mAP@0.5 on held-out scenes fell from 0.12 to
0.06, but the healthy-frame false-positive rate fell from 45 % to 17 % at a 0.25 floor (30 % → 8 % at 0.35). At
the track level, 0.35 gives 8/11 defects with 0 phantoms versus the baseline's 9/11 with 8. The fine-tuned model
is deployed with a 0.35 floor. Lesson: select the detector by the metric of the system it sits in. Caveat:
unlabelled damage inside mined crops becomes wrong negatives, which probably explains part of the recall loss.

## 9. Dashboard

Built to answer, in order: *is anything about to fail, where, and what should I do?* Then: *what is abnormal
now?* Then: *what is wrong with the belt surface?* Then: *can I trust the data?*

* **Belt map:** the true route (elevation, curves, both strands) with every station at its real chainage.
  Clustered stations are spread out with leader lines. Status uses colour **plus icon plus label**: normal ✓,
  abnormal !, predicted failure ✕, no data ?. A station that stopped reporting is never shown as normal. A
  table view mirrors the map.
* **Predictions:** probability, failure mode, location, median ETA with a calibrated range, and a recommended
  action tied to the planned-stop schedule ("fix in planned stop Sun 00:00" vs "inspect now").
* **Active anomalies:** severity, type, location, duration, evidence in engineering units.
* **Vision:** the belt loop as a strip in belt coordinates with splices; confirmed vs unconfirmed defects; crops;
  sightings vs times imaged; where to find each defect if the belt is stopped.
* **Station detail:** small multiples with one y-axis each and a shared 72 h window. Stopped periods are shaded.
* **Data quality** and the **maintenance log**.

Dataviz rules followed: one axis per chart, no colour-only encoding, reserved status palette, hairline grids,
text in ink colours, tooltips that never gate information, light and dark palettes.

**Replay mode.** The same page can run without a backend: `scripts/record_replay.py` runs the real system on a
fixed simulated clock and records every API response each simulated half hour into `docs/demo/`. With
`window.BELT_REPLAY` set, the dashboard answers its API calls from those frames and adds play, pause and scrub
controls. GitHub Pages serves it as a static site, so reviewers can try the system without installing anything.
It is labelled as a recording everywhere it appears.

**Image inspection page (`docs/inspect/`).** A reviewer can also try the vision component on their own photos.
Both detectors are exported to ONNX and run in the browser with ONNX Runtime Web (WebAssembly, single thread,
because GitHub Pages is not cross-origin isolated). There is no server, so no upload endpoint to secure and no
images leave the device. The live backend serves the same page at `/inspect/`.

The page re-implements Ultralytics' pre- and post-processing in JavaScript:
* letterbox to 512 px with grey padding;
* best class per anchor;
* per-class greedy NMS at IoU 0.5 (Ultralytics' predict default, not the 0.7 used in validation);
* rescale and clip.

`vision/export_web.py --check` runs the same steps in numpy against Ultralytics on the same ONNX file and gets
identical boxes and confidences on 78 images. I checked the browser against Python separately in headless
Chromium:
* the JPEG decode is bit-identical;
* the canvas resize differs from OpenCV's by under one grey level on 99 % of pixels;
* detections at the 0.35 threshold match on 66 of 69 images (deployed) and 68 of 69 (baseline), with a median
  confidence difference under 0.01.

Every mismatch was a borderline box, at the confidence threshold or at the NMS overlap limit. Long thin tears
also moved their box end by tens of pixels under these sub-grey-level changes. That is a robustness finding about
the detector, not a page bug.

The page shows single-image detections only. It keeps the class-based severity and states that on the belt an
alert needs multi-pass confirmation, with size and growth feeding severity. Sample images carry the dataset's
labels as dashed boxes, so the detector's misses are visible next to its hits.

## 10. What is a tool, what is learned, what would be an agent

| Concern | Implemented as | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ingestion, validation, units, timestamps, dedup | Deterministic code | Must be exact, auditable, testable |
| Data-quality flags (spikes, stuck, offline) | Deterministic rules | Explainable; standard practice |
| Normalisation and anomaly detection | Statistics (peer + frozen self-baseline, robust z) | Needs no failure labels; explainable to a maintenance planner |
| Bearing and mistracking prediction | Learned (gradient boosting + conformal quantiles) | Population of ~24 similar assets yields enough failures |
| Drive prediction | Physics-informed trend extrapolation | One asset: too few failures to learn from |
| Damage detection in images | Learned (YOLO11n) | The only practical way to find damage in pixels |
| Belt-loop localisation and association | Deterministic geometry + tracking | It is a geometry problem; learning it would be worse and opaque |
| Prioritisation, recommended action | Rules on top of predictions + schedule | Deterministic and auditable |
| **LLM agent** | **Not in the monitoring path** | Non-deterministic, latency, offline mine sites, safety. The right place for one is *on top of* these tools: a shift-handover summary or a work-order draft that calls the same `/api/*` endpoints the dashboard uses, with a human approving. Not built |

## 11. Production path (what changes for real)

* **Edge and transport:** industrial gateways per radio segment, MQTT with Sparkplug B, store-and-forward,
  NTP/PTP time sync, an OT/IT boundary per IEC 62443 (ideally a one-way data diode).
* **Storage and compute:** TimescaleDB (hypertables, continuous aggregates) or InfluxDB instead of SQLite. A
  message broker (Kafka/MQTT) between ingestion and analysis. Analysis workers partitioned by conveyor. Object
  storage for crops.
* **Model lifecycle:** labels come from CMMS work orders (failure codes, replaced parts). This is the hard part
  and needs a process, not code. "Confirm / false alarm" buttons on alerts feed back as labels. Retrain monthly,
  shadow-deploy, and monitor feature drift. Per-site calibration of thresholds and conformal widths.
* **Alarm management:** ISA-18.2 / EEMUA 191 practices (alarm rates, priorities, shelving, flood suppression).
* **Vision hardware:** line-scan camera with LED lighting and an air knife against dust, an RFID reader at the
  camera, an edge GPU, splice detection fused with the RFID reads.

## 12. Limitations (honest list)

* The sensor models are trained and evaluated on **my own simulator**. The numbers show that the pipeline works
  and how the methods compare under controlled, messy conditions. They are not a real-world accuracy estimate.
  The relative comparison (late fixed limits, noisy z-scores under bad data) is the transferable part.
* Simulator physics, P-F intervals, hazards and signature strengths are assumptions. Real data will have
  failure modes the simulator does not imagine.
* The vision dataset is still photos, not video of the same belt, so the loop behaviour is simulated by
  re-rendering the same photo each revolution. Defect *growth* cannot be observed from real pixels here.
* The detector is weak on unseen scenes (mAP@0.5 0.12 baseline, 0.06 for the deployed hard-negative model, which
  trades box quality for far fewer false positives). Its false-positive rate on
  healthy belt, multiplied by ~12,050 frames per revolution, would confirm phantom defects at full coverage. The
  simulation images only a sample of healthy frames per revolution, so the system as demonstrated would not yet
  hold up on a real belt without site-specific training data (§8.4).
* One conveyor, one site, one camera. Fleet-wide models and transfer between conveyors are out of scope.
